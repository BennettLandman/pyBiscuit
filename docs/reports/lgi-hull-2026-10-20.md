Analytic: surface = hull gives LGI = 1 to 1e-12; median LGI is strictly increasing over fold amplitudes
0.03, 0.08, 0.14, 0.2 (hull fixed); the libstdc++ heap variant agrees with libc++ on the tie-free fixture.

**Root cause of the Linux CI numerics failure (PR #6 run 39537417007).** The goldens were produced with Apple's
`acosf`; MeshLib weights face normals by float32 `acosf`, a different `acosf` rounds ~8 % of inputs 1 ulp differently,
and the kernel areas flip membership at the cumulative-area cutoff under such a change (emulated: +-1 ulp on the
normals gives 70-80 of 2,562 vertices off by up to 3 %, the CI signature; 1e-13 perturbations of areas/distances/tensors
give none). Fix: the `acosf` of the normal weights is pinned and selectable (`libm="fdlibm"` default, `"host"`), the
oracle build links the same fdlibm algorithm, goldens regenerated; nothing was loosened. The pin is **provisional**: whether
the reference image's glibc `acosf` equals fdlibm is unverified until image goldens exist (B1.4); the switch is
`DEFAULT_LIBM` in `quant/_libm.py`. Details: `docs/ports/lgi.md`, "libm".

**Finding (ties).** On the bare icosphere with a mirror-symmetric bumpy function (the existing smoke fixture) three vertices
are exactly equidistant from two curve vertices. Which source claims them depends on `std::heap` layout (libc++ in the
oracle, libstdc++ in the Linux image), the nearest-source id feeds `maxDist` in the tensor field, and 96 of 10,242 vertices
got different kernel membership (kernel area up to 2 %, LGI unchanged to 5e-7 only because the hull was capped). Real
cortical meshes have no exact ties. The port implements both heap flavours (`heap=` / `--heap`); the libstdc++ one is untested
against a real Linux binary.

### OuterHull

No oracle: MATLAB absent, Octave not installable (the conda-forge channel is unreachable from the work machine). The port
follows the `.m` files line by line where they are self-contained (VOXELISE, ICP_finite, fminlbfgs, arithmetic of the script)
and re-derives MATLAB library calls: `strel('ball',15,15)` exact ellipsoid per z-slice (MATLAB: N = 8 line decomposition),
`isosurface` by marching tetrahedra, largest component instead of the first `dmperm` block. See `docs/ports/outer-hull.md`.
Behaviour on real data: `reg/mb101/lh.white.vtk` (163,842 vertices, 158 mm long): 4.1 s, hull 490,926 vertices / 981,848 faces,
genus 0; mesh-to-hull vertex distance median 2.2 mm, p99 13.1 mm; hull area 51,786 mm² (surface 52,827, convex hull 33,690). The
area grows with grid resolution (43.9k at 64, 46.1k at 128) because the closing radius is fixed in voxels (15 voxels = 9.3 mm
at 256), as in the script. Whether this matches the image's hull cannot be said without an image run; because LGI kernel sizes
scale with the hull area (`--refHullArea 77100`), an area bias in the hull translates into a kernel-size bias.

4. The brief 03 policy calls for ties to be explained; here the fixtures are made tie-free instead. Exact-tie inputs are flagged in
   the port note, not tested.
